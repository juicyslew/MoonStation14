# H2 — vanilla inventory isolation route matrix

**Status: read-only bounded audit and design proposal. No lifecycle owner or runtime behavior was changed.** Inspection used the checked-in `build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.224-sources.jar` (NeoForge 21.1.224 / Minecraft 1.21.1) plus the current controller and item sources. This is not an implementation approval, exhaustive modded-route proof, crash-safe persistence proof, or connected acceptance report.

## Decision / invariants carried forward

The already-approved ownership policy is:

Existing vanilla items from actual Creative are account-owned. While an eligible non-Creative body is controlled, preserve/quarantine those items separately and restore the exact items **only on actual return to Creative**—also after body death and disconnect/reconnect. Never copy them into SS14 hands, equipment, or item containers; never drop them on character death or lose them. This is an owner decision, not evidence that quarantine or recovery is implemented. A durable quarantine journal and crash-recovery/reconciliation design remain open engineering work and block activation.

| Carrier state | Owner of vanilla player inventory | Intended access |
|---|---|---|
| Actual `CREATIVE`, with no active body session | Vanilla player / Creative | Full vanilla Creative inventory. No body-owned items are copied into it. |
| `SURVIVAL` and admitted eligible SS14 character body | Account-owned vanilla items are preserved in separate quarantine; carrier slots are unavailable to gameplay | SS14 body owns only its defined hands and named equipment. Storage remains item-owned. No vanilla Survival inventory as a second owner; quarantined vanilla items are not copied into body holdings. |
| `SPECTATOR`, without an admitted active character body (including ghost-only, pending handoff, or unrelated spectator) | Existing carrier/playerdata behavior | Do not silently clear, park, or change it under this feature. Ordinary vanilla Spectator restrictions still apply. |

An active committed harness currently requires a Spectator carrier; therefore its normal committed state and actual Creative are mutually exclusive. A carrier must never get a second Survival inventory by treating “Spectator” as permission to use vanilla slots. Conversely, vanilla Spectator restrictions are *not* isolation: they are mode-dependent affordances, do not erase or quarantine stacks, and leave direct server mutation routes.

## Current owner seams and missing resolver coverage

- `GhostMobHarnessControl.activeHarness(ServerPlayer)` / `activeCharacterBody` (`src/main/java/.../server/GhostMobHarnessControl.java:929–959`) is read-only and admits its own exact connected, non-fake, committed, living, Spectator session with matching registry mind/harness/epoch and a currently eligible ghost or character. Ghost sessions return no character body. It does not authorize lifecycle-controller sessions.
- `LifecycleCharacterSessionControl` (`src/main/java/.../lifecycle/server/LifecycleCharacterSessionControl.java`) separately holds first-join lifecycle `Session`s and validates lifecycle profile, generation, exact body, account binding, and lifecycle registry. `ownsAny` is package-private and backed by another map. Its committed camera check is not an inventory/body resolver. It currently requires Spectator at preflight and commits after Ready.
- Consequently, calling `activeCharacterBody` for inventory admission misses the `LifecycleCharacterSessionControl` character population; broadening `GhostMobHarnessControl` opportunistically is also wrong because its registry, eligibility, and cleanup ownership differ. A unified **read-only exact owner seam** must be coordinated with both lifecycle/controller owners: authenticated exact carrier, session state committed (not merely prepared), body kind/identity, epoch/generation, current liveness/eligibility, mode, and rejection reason. It must not revoke, transfer, or mutate either lifecycle owner.
- During `LifecycleDevelopmentMode` Creative detach/return, `parkForDevelopmentMode` durably parks the active lifecycle session and `DETACHED` tracks an operator carrier; neither method snapshots or clears vanilla inventory. `DETACHED` is ephemeral and cleared on logout/server stop. This existing development route is not an inventory transition implementation.
- Admission must be **active eligible character body only**. Ghost-only, pending offer/return, Begin/Ready handshake, detached Creative carrier, lifecycle ghost, stale owner record, and any non-active ghost session are not body inventory owners. Fail closed for body authority, but preserve their existing carrier inventory rather than clearing it. Do not infer body ownership solely from Spectator mode, a character attachment, an account profile, or “some session exists.”

## Parking strategies

### A. Snapshot vanilla inventory, then clear on entry

Preserve every account-owned Creative stack and relevant inventory metadata in an exact-account quarantine record; clear carrier slots and cursor/menu state before eligible non-Creative body authority. Restore the exact parked snapshot only on actual return to Creative, including after body death and disconnect/reconnect. Never move these stacks into SS14 body holdings or drop them on character death. This owner-mandated outcome is subject to the durable-journal activation blocker below; it is not a claim that the transaction is implemented or currently safe.

*Advantages:* real physical absence from player slots blocks ordinary and many accidental routes; there is one obvious quarantine payload and conservation can be tested.
*Risks:* snapshot/clear and restore/save are multi-object transactions; inventory APIs may alias mutable `ItemStack`s; creative-mode transition events may run at the wrong edge; cursor/open menu and armor/offhand/ender or mod-added compartments need scope decisions; any record loss loses items; stale client menu state can replay after restoration.

### B. Leave carrier inventory in situ, quarantine access

Retain inventory physically on carrier while an ownership policy blocks every route and hides it from the controlled body.

*Advantages:* no stack copy/move at the initial transition and no separate snapshot to restore.
*Risks:* cannot be complete at packet boundary: `handlePickItem` bypasses Spectator, server commands, `getInventory().add`, item callbacks, clone/save, and external mods reach it. Client may observe stale slots; any miss leaks/mutates the second inventory. Access control also becomes global invasive policy across all ways into `Inventory`.

**Recommendation:** only strategy A is a credible path to physical isolation, but it is not approved as safe until durable transaction and lifecycle ownership are agreed and tested. Strategy B is not acceptable as the claimed final isolation. A minimal test of a standalone copy/clear helper does not authorize enabling either strategy.

## Required transition protocol (proposed, unresolved durability boundary)

Use a server-thread transaction coordinator keyed to exact account UUID **and** a transition/session generation. Never trust client-provided body IDs or generations. All checks and operations below must occur against the same exact authenticated `ServerPlayer` and current owner result; reject if the owner seam returns non-active/ambiguous.

### Preflight (no mutation)

1. Confirm exact connected non-fake player object on expected server thread, current game mode and requested transition, exact committed eligible CHARACTER owner, current profile/body binding and epoch/generation, no passenger/death/removal, no concurrent transition, and allowed policy (no Creative+body overlap).
2. Inspect the *entire approved vanilla carrier inventory scope* (main/hotbar, armor, offhand, cursor/carried item, and any agreed extra compartments), selected index, open menu identity/state, and pending client/menu transaction state. Inventory must be serializable and all destinations/recovery capacity guaranteed. Do not assume empty.
3. Establish an immutable deep stack snapshot (including components/count and slot positions), digest/transaction ID, owner generation, and prior/current mode. Validate uniqueness and no pre-existing unresolved quarantine record. If any item is unsupported, a menu cannot be safely reset, or persistence is unavailable, reject before mutation and keep prior mode/session.
4. For actual return to Creative, preflight that the exact matching account quarantine record exists, is complete and unconsumed, player slots are clear of conflicting items, mode request is authorized, and clone/relogin recovery does not create a second owner. Do not restore merely because the body died, and do not merge “helpfully.”

### Commit and rollback (one server-thread critical section, durable journal required)

1. Reserve a unique transaction; block inventory packets, command mutation, pickups, action/use, and direct project-controlled grants for the exact player from before the first write until commit/abort. Close or invalidate current menu and synchronize authoritative empty/menu state. Late packets must not fall through to vanilla handlers.
2. **Entry to eligible body:** durably write a `PREPARED` quarantine journal with exact deep snapshot and owner/session generation; flush/atomic-replace it and verify readback before removing any stack. Then clear every inventoried slot/cursor and verify every slot empty. Only after clear verification mark journal `PARKED` durable; then permit body session commit/eligible state. The controlled body must not become active before this point.
3. **Exit to actual Creative:** keep vanilla inventory empty and block mutations while restore is underway. Verify `PARKED` journal exact owner/generation; write durable `RESTORING` intent; install snapshot into exact original slots, verifying each stack; only then durably mark `RESTORED`/consumed and allow Creative access. If any step fails, clear partial restore only if it can be proved safe, otherwise keep access blocked and require recovery. Do not delete the only durable copy before verified installation and durable consumption state.
4. Rollback on synchronous failure: retain the journal; restore exact original mode and owner only if it is still exact/current, reverse all writes from journal, verify inventory equality and no drops/duplicates, then mark abort. If exact rollback cannot be proven, leave player disconnected/locked from both vanilla inventory and body control and retain recoverable journal for operator recovery. Never “best effort” merge.
5. On success, release packet/action barrier only after authority and empty/restore state are committed; resync inventory, selected slot, carried stack, active container, and mode to client. Idempotency key is transaction ID + account + generation; repeated begin/commit/return does not copy again.

**Atomicity limitation:** a normal Minecraft playerdata save and a mod-owned quarantine file are separate durable writes. An in-memory map, event ordering, or synchronized method is not crash atomic. Even journal-before-clear has a crash interval where playerdata still contains original inventory while the journal also contains a copy; recovery must suppress loaded inventory before any use and reconcile one authoritative copy. If clear/playerdata save can lag or journal update can be lost, naive recovery duplicates. A crash after inventory clear but before playerdata save risks the inverse. No current code establishes this cross-file transaction or guarantees save hooks run at a safe boundary. Do not claim lossless crash recovery until a write-ahead, replay-safe protocol is implemented and power-loss tested; otherwise disable feature or stop server transition with explicit recovery, not silent fallback.

## Lifecycle, replay, and save boundaries

- **Late/replay packets:** put a server-side isolation barrier ahead of vanilla mutation for all covered handlers, including `handlePickItem` and `handleSetCreativeModeSlot`; check current exact owner + generation at processing time, not packet arrival time. While transaction pending, reject and resend authoritative inventory/menu state. Discard stale menu container IDs/state IDs and any packet queued across mode switch. For non-active ghost/pending sessions, the feature must not claim inventory ownership; unrelated vanilla semantics remain.
- **Direct grants and commands:** guard known project APIs (including Cable Cutter result path) and item drop fallback with the same ownership decision. Audit all local add/set/drop calls and NeoForge insertion hooks. Define commands explicitly; do not allow `/give` or equivalent to seed carrier inventory during body control. External mods calling `getInventory()` remain a stated gap until centrally intercepted or excluded by support policy.
- **Disconnect/reconnect:** do not clear the only copy on logout. Persist `PARKED` journal before eligible admission. On login, inspect durable record before a body becomes active or inventory can be interacted with; reconcile vanilla loaded slots against journal by exact generation and digest, then keep items quarantined while non-Creative body control continues, or restore them only on actual Creative return. If state is ambiguous, keep blocked/disconnect and retain recovery data. Do not use stale in-memory exact-player identity after reconnect.
- **Death/clone:** account-owned vanilla contents remain quarantined through controlled-body death and are restored only on actual Creative return; character death must not drop or dispose of them. Coordinate with lifecycle death claim/ghost handoff. `restoreFrom(..., keepEverything)` may copy vanilla inventory to replacement; intercept/reconcile clone before active admission and make old snapshot, old player, corpse drops, and clone mutually exclusive owners. No automatic keep/drop/duplicate path is acceptable. Durable clone/death recovery mechanics remain unresolved and block activation.
- **Creative entry/exit:** Creative entry from an active lifecycle character already detaches durable lifecycle state before mode change; add quarantine coordination before detachment/Creative permission. Returning via `/ms14dev return` currently reactivates lifecycle ownership but does not clear/park Creative inventory first. Preflight/park Creative inventory before body activation, and restore quarantined vanilla stacks only when leaving body for actual Creative. Creative command access and operator status do not waive item conservation.
- **Spectator:** mode alone is not a feature state. Existing non-active Spectator and ghost carrier retains its inventory/save behavior. Active CHARACTER Spectator may be the session transport/camera carrier, but its vanilla inventory must be physically empty/quarantined before body commit. Handle unauthorized switch to Survival/Creative by withholding both modes’ inventory access until coordinated transition; current `activeHarness` ceases to resolve in non-Spectator and may then appear absent, so it cannot alone protect this interval.
- **Save boundary / shutdown:** before playerdata save, ensure no active isolated carrier has inventory in both live slots and journal and journal is durable; save hook ordering must be verified against actual `PlayerList` save flow and NeoForge events. Shutdown drains only after durable journal state is verified. Save/load, disconnect, server stop, crash restart, and clone all require recovery tests; no existing lifecycle save hook provides the inventory transaction automatically.

## Practical staged implementation and gates

Do **not** ship only a `handleContainerClick`/Creative-slot mixin or a packet gate and describe it as isolation. A safely shippable first runtime tranche is an end-to-end **disabled-by-default** transition slice, only after owner review:

1. Coordinate and add one read-only combined body-owner resolver spanning both debug harness and lifecycle character owners, with typed reasoned rejection and generation; no mutation/revocation side effects. Keep non-active ghost and pending states explicitly excluded.
2. Implement/test the durable quarantine journal and pure preflight/commit/recovery state machine against deep snapshots, item conservation, idempotent replay, interrupted writes, and ambiguous recovery. Confirm playerdata save/clone integration or leave activation hard-disabled.
3. In the same gated tranche, wire entry/exit lifecycle transitions **and** the complete verified route barrier set (at minimum menu/cursor/drag/button/recipe, pick-item, creative slot, drop/offhand, pickup, use/use-on/interact, command/direct grant seams, save/login/clone). If any route or mode transition is absent, keep the feature gate off; do not enable a partial barrier.
4. Dedicated-server tests: existing Creative remains vanilla; controlled eligible CHARACTER gets an empty carrier only after durable park and cannot mutate it through each matrix route; unrelated Survival and non-active Spectator/ghost cases are unchanged; exact rollback on injected failure; no loss/duplication across Creative↔body, reconnect, death/clone, save/reload, stale/late packets, and crash points. Then owner smoke/two-client acceptance is a separate required gate.

This order allows early pure resolver/journal test work to merge safely, but no partial packet gate is an independently shippable isolation feature. Until end-to-end transitions, recovery, and route coverage pass, keep activation disabled and do not assert that eligible bodies have no vanilla inventory.

## Decisions requiring owner review

1. Confirm admitted population: both committed debug CHARACTER and lifecycle CHARACTER, or lifecycle-only first; explicitly exclude ghost-only, pending, detached Creative, and stale sessions.
2. Define exact quarantine scope and engineering details (armor/offhand/cursor/selected slot, modded player compartments, ender chest, recipe/book state); the owner outcome is settled: preserve account-owned Creative items separately and restore only on actual Creative return, including after body death and reconnect, never copying to body containers, dropping on character death, or losing them.
3. Choose persistence owner/storage and implement/validate crash recovery authority across playerdata + quarantine journal; identify save/clone interception seam and supported external-mod contract. This remains open and blocks activation.
4. Specify and validate disconnect, death, clone, profile reset/deletion, and server-shutdown recovery consistent with the settled preserve-and-restore outcome. Do not interpret this engineering gap as permission to drop, delete, merge, or lose parked stacks.
5. Define commands and operator/dev-mode behavior; ensure Creative entry/return cannot overlap body ownership or leak a stale cursor/menu packet.
6. Coordinate the combined resolver and transition transaction seam with lifecycle/controller owners; confirm whether to modify their ownership flow or expose narrowly scoped callbacks. This audit does not authorize owner changes.
