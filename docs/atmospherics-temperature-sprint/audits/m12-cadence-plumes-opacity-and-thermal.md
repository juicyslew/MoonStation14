## 1. Atmosphere and device cadence

`AtmosphereService.TICK_CADENCE` is currently two Minecraft game ticks: 10 service invocations per second at 20 TPS. This replaces the prior four-game-tick cadence. LINDA is capped at 10 active sources and six face checks per source, with a maximum of 64 face checks per due step (thus at most 60 from the source cap). The relevant per-invocation budgets were halved against that earlier cadence to preserve approximate per-second counts; this is a throughput estimate, not proof of equivalent load or bounded tick latency. In particular, an 800-cell patch write may still produce a concentrated one-tick cost. Server TPS, measured atmosphere cost, and the 20-player target remain unverified.

Devices also use the two-tick step. At 20 TPS, producer and proportional sink are each capped at 2 mol per step (20 mol/s); production remains pressure-capped at 202.65 kPa and may take a partial dose. Heater/cooler energy is 40,000 J per step (400,000 J/s); cooler has a 2.7 K floor. These figures describe source rules and nominal 20-TPS conversion, not observed rates under lag or server-world acceptance. Older statements describing a one-second device interval or 40,000 J/s are stale.

SS14's configured 15 TPS is not directly comparable to this cadence: it advances atmosphere in stages, not a full LINDA plus Monstermos pass 15 times per second. The local 10 Hz cycle is not upstream parity. Excited groups advance on a separate 12 Minecraft game-tick interval (0.6 seconds at 20 TPS, or six service due cycles); any delay caused by same-cycle patch changes can shift that interval. Do not describe local work as one upstream-equivalent full solve per update.

## 2. Historical normal Monstermos plume and excited-group notes (normal route superseded)

- Normal Monstermos transfers at most 0.15 of each donor's initial surplus per due step and retains half of a packet in an intermediate cell on long routes. These are deliberate deviations from the upstream tendency to attempt the full surplus, chosen to make visible, gradual plumes instead of abrupt whole-route redistribution. Patch limits (800 applied cells / 8,000 discovery candidates), route work limits, and partial progress still apply. A visually gradual plume is not pressure-wind physics; no entity impulse or decompression force is implemented.
- Excited-group breakdown after the fifth counted full group pass no longer implies unconditional immediate averaging. Breakdown is permitted only when each tracked cell is within 5% of standard 41.6 mol per 1 m³ cell (about 2.08 mol) for each species relative to the group average, and within 4 K of average temperature. If those checks fail, breakdown is deferred; do not claim a group always snaps uniformly on pass five. Dismantling remains after the seventeenth counted pass.
- Monstermos space flow sends 0.15 of the available gas per step and uses the same 1e-6 mol epsilon; it has no visible hard-zero/refill-to-zero behavior. The immutable finite-to-vacuum fallback used when a Monstermos job is unavailable behaves differently: above 10 kPa pressure difference it removes enough gas for half the pressure difference, while at or below 10 kPa it drains half the gas per step, snapping residual inventory to vacuum only at or below 1e-6 mol. Thus it does not apply SS14's immediate two-mol minimum-inventory cleanup. In both paths ledger gas and heat deltas are exact for the before/after finite-cell states; the space ledger remains transient. These gradual behaviors avoid an immediate two-mol cliff but allow remnants to decay over later processing while connected room gas remains. The pressure/release approximations do not supply pressure wind.

These rules are deliberate Minecraft behavior deviations, not SS14 equivalence. Closed-path gas and energy accounting remains kernel behavior, while space export is represented in the transient level ledger; ledger persistence and whole-world conservation across lifecycle/topology events remain open questions.

## 3. Gas overlay concentration, alpha, and ignition non-claims

**Historical M12 visual decision — superseded.** At M12 this audit recorded a widened 40 mol-per-block saturation cap, a nonzero alpha immediately above threshold, and a 0.55 maximum column opacity. The user rejected the widened cap/block and requested restoration of the pre-M12 visibility range. Those historical values are not current behavior; cadence, plume, group and thermal findings in this audit remain current.

**Current gas visibility policy (confirmed against `GasVisibility.java` and `GasVisibilityTest.java`):** SS14 reference maxima are 5 mol for default overlay gases, 7 mol ammonia, and 12 mol frezon in a 2.5 m³ reference cell; the reference thresholds and maxima scale by volume to the Minecraft 1 m³ cell. Thus default gases (plasma, tritium and water vapor) use threshold/maximum 0.1/2 mol, ammonia 0.8/2.8 mol, and frezon 0.24/4.8 mol. Only plasma, tritium, water vapor, ammonia and frezon have overlays; nitrous oxide does not. The original rounded 20-level alpha quantization remains: values at/below threshold return alpha 0 and a value just above threshold can also quantize to alpha 0. The renderer uses three crossed planes and limits the compounded four-block column to 0.85 opacity; crossed quads are inset to a maximum 0.48-block radius. Visibility selection is nearest-first, with tritium favored only for equal-distance candidates. The visual data continues to use non-reset deltas; reset snapshots, staged client cache publication, and resynchronization on bounded tracking overflow are unchanged. No SS14 PNG/sprite was copied; the tint remains a procedural UI-color approximation rather than the upstream white-alpha sprite/animation. Actual readability and compounded appearance remain unverified in-game.

The upstream `Resources/Prototypes/Atmospherics/reactions.yml` `TritiumFire` reaction has a minimum of 0.01 mol tritium and 0.01 mol oxygen per 2.5 m³ cell at or above 373.149 K. By concentration, 1 mol in a Minecraft 1 m³ cell corresponds to 2.5 mol per SS14 2.5 m³ tile, far above that minimum. This comparison does **not** imply ignition: reaction conditions also require oxygen and heat, and this project does not implement fire reactions. Overlay visibility/opacity has no ignition-safety meaning, does not indicate a safe concentration, and does not cause chemical or physiological damage. Gas composition effects, including tritium/fire harm, remain unimplemented.

## 4. References and acceptance status
