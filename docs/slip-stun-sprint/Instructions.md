## 1. Goal, scope, and requirements

Deliver a Minecraft/NeoForge adaptation of pinned SS14 slippery-puddle behavior: source- and target-defined slip behavior, a real timed server stun with investigated knockdown/crawling, and **required slip-triggered reactive Touch effects**. Pinned SS14 behavior is normative. Do not replace it with a novel prototype-only outcome model. If a notable Minecraft limitation forces a behavioral deviation, stop, provide verified evidence and narrow alternatives with a recommendation, and obtain the owner's decision before deviating.

### Character identity is a long-term universal architecture

Ultimately every `LivingEntity` is associated with a character prototype: player characters, animals, hostile mobs, zombies, every humanoid race, and future characters. Every character must be controllable by a player **or** AI; controller possession, control transfer, and switching never redefine its prototype or physical policy. Some future sentient/AI-capable actors may not be `LivingEntity` at all and will need an explicit nonliving character adapter. `LivingEntity` describes today's Minecraft technical basis, not a blanket slip rule or the complete future character taxonomy. No entity slips merely because it is living.

Rollout may be incremental, but this is identity architecture, not a permanent opt-in enclave. The initial proof must bind a real player character and a real Minecraft villager NPC to the same prototype and demonstrate shared policy, showing that policy is independent of whether the actor is a player or villager. This does not require possession or control transfer: the repository has no controller possession/transfer system. M0/M1 must also establish a concrete, owned coverage plan, adapters for additional actor families, and a dated coverage-review/deadline gate. **Do not require global conversion of all populations in this sprint.** Unbound actors remain inert while rollout proceeds, but the plan must not treat them as permanently outside the character model.

### In scope

- Typed character prototype family, server-authoritative identity binding, and incremental coverage plan.
- Prototype-defined target susceptibility/status/protection capabilities and shared player/AI action authorization.
- Reagent/puddle source data and mixture aggregation consistent with pinned SS14 semantics, including the owner-approved canonical reagent JSON `slipData` source schema.
- A verified authoritative Minecraft contact/step trigger, timed real server action blocking/stun, and an honest prone/crawl feasibility investigation.
- **Required** reactive Touch admission and transfer downstream of a real slip, matching upstream eligibility, timing, chance, and dose semantics as closely as local capabilities permit.
- Focused tests, acceptance report, and explicit deviations/limitations.
- Bounded M5 Minecraft grounded sliding adapter: source friction from current puddle contents, `F`-scaled grounded acceleration, `pow(vanillaRetention, F)` horizontal retention, and synchronized nonpersistent sliding state; connected-client movement/synchronization remains owner-verified.

### Out of scope

Equipment/armor handling is entirely out of scope: add no armor prototypes, vanilla armor changes, slot schemas, equipment permission logic, or equipment-slot implementation. Keep only a minimal extensible slip-attempt/no-slip capability hook for a future equipment sprint; intrinsic character-prototype no-slip policy is testable now. No general character rewrite, all-entity ECS conversion, full status/chemistry rewrite, or literal SS14 ECS port. True physical crawling may require a later sprint, but inability to implement it does not silently cancel truthful limited stun/slip work; it requires an explicit milestone decision and honest report. Do not call marker-only knockdown full SS14 knockdown. No manual game launch by the implementation worker.

### Pinned SS14 behavior (normative reference)

Reference checkout: `C:\Users\William\Documents\SS14-dev\space-station-14`, pinned HEAD `c9df5ef5d675b0d1d226828bddf6b78c28502d91`.

- `Content.Shared/Slippery/SlipperySystem.cs:92-155`: `CanSlip` rejects contained targets and requires the target to be able to receive stun status. `TrySlip` handles already-knocked-down targets (ordinary slippery sources do not retrigger; super-slippery can), raises target `SlipAttemptEvent` (including `NoSlip` and slow-over-slippery response), then source `SlipCausingAttemptEvent` (cancellable). Once admitted it raises `SlipEvent` **before** launch velocity, sliding, hand drop, stun/friction/stamina/audio, and forced knockdown. The SlipEvent is an attempt that passed those gates; it is not a post-status commit notification. Puddle reactive handling subscribed to this event consequently runs before those later motion/status operations. Do not move effects after a locally invented status-commit event unless a verified Minecraft limitation requires it and the owner approves.
- `Content.Shared/Fluids/SharedPuddleSystem.cs:228-320` (and `LowThreshold = 0.3`): a small puddle (`solution.Volume <= overflowVolume * 0.3`) is disabled. For each reagent, minimum slip speed and friction are accumulated weighted by its quantity; slip speed and friction are averaged over total solution volume. A reagent without slip data contributes the puddle's default slippery speed but not slippery volume. Slip launch multiplier, stun time, knockdown time, and super-slippery quantity are weighted only over reagents with `SlipData`. The step trigger is active only when total slippery-reagent volume is **strictly greater than** `overflowVolume * 0.3`; super-slippery is enabled when its own volume is **greater than or equal to** that cutoff. The source's required speed is compared by the step-trigger system. These pinned default/cutoff and weighted-aggregation semantics are the reference; identify unit/precision differences rather than silently changing the rules.
- `Content.Server/Fluids/EntitySystems/PuddleSystem.cs:245-268`: on `SlipEvent`, the source puddle handler first requires a target with Reactive and excludes a target already sliding at that event time, rolls a 50% chance, resolves the actual current solution, splits 15% of that solution, then dispatches `Touch`. This split occurs before per-reagent `ReactiveSystem` group/method filtering, so a reagent with no matching group/method can still be part of the consumed split even though it dispatches no effect. Failed target eligibility/chance/solution resolution spends no reagent; a per-reagent method/group mismatch is not an upstream zero-dose condition. Effects are not always-on `entityInside` behavior. Reuse defaults 50% and 15%; do not turn them into owner questions or configurable knobs absent a demonstrated Minecraft constraint.
- Reactive dispatch must also preserve upstream method/group filtering: target reactive-group admission for `TOUCH` and reagent reactive-effects method admission are both required; dispatch supported per-reagent Touch reactions with the actual split quantity as effect scale.
- `ReagentUnits` uses integer hundredths and truncates numeric ingress to cents. PuddleSystem (`Content.Server/Fluids/EntitySystems/PuddleSystem.cs:265-267`) requests `solution.Volume * 0.15f` and passes it to shared upstream `SplitSolution`; `FixedPoint2 * float` (`Content.Shared/FixedPoint/FixedPoint2.cs:101-104`) applies a small signed epsilon before truncation. Use pinned rounding for the 15% request by default, subject to golden tests of requested/taken doses and per-reagent portions (mixed pools, varied ordering, and edge/tiny-cent amounts). Align any actual common-split disparity as described above after assessing caller impact; do not use binary-float per-reagent removal.
- Upstream executes `SlipEvent` before the remainder of slip processing and the puddle handler removes/splits solution before dispatching Touch effects. This is intentionally not an atomic transaction spanning callbacks, statuses, motion, reagent mutation, and arbitrary world effects. Preserve the normal split-before-effects failure boundary; report/contain per-effect failures without falsely promising rollback or replaying dose.
- Pinned target evidence: `Resources/Prototypes/Body/Species/human.yml:89-99` defines `MobHuman`, inheriting `BaseSpeciesMob` (`Resources/Prototypes/Body/species_base.yml:1-10`), whose base includes Reactive Touch groups (`:62-71`), StandingState (`:106-107`), and MobState via BaseMob. `StatusEffectStunned` is whitelisted for MobState and blacklists StunImmune (`Resources/Prototypes/Entities/StatusEffects/movement.yml:67-81`). Derive eligibility from these inherited components; do not assert upstream has a serialized `slip_data` field or invent a status name. Pinned `MobPig`, `MobChicken`, and `MobCow` inherit `SimpleMobBase` -> StunImmune (`Resources/Prototypes/Entities/Mobs/NPCs/simplemob.yml:27-31`): they are not positive fixtures, and do not override immunity to make them slip.

## 3. Character prototype and source design

### Typed character family and binding

Add an immutable validated `CharacterData` family, `PrototypeType<CharacterData>`, catalog registration through current `PrototypeRuntime`/reload paths, and a server-side key-only identity binding attachment/system. Sprint prototype minimum: one typed default-human prototype, e.g. `moonstation14:human`, with an explicit typed `slip_data` field/capability. Treat `slip_data` as a minimal target-capability grouping, not source reagent data: it groups (1) ability to receive stun/status, (2) intrinsic `NoSlip`, (3) standing/prone eligibility, and (4) reactive target Touch group and method admission. Represent these through supported local typed fields/enums and map them to derived pinned-component equivalents (MobState eligibility vs StunImmune, StandingState/prone state, Reactive Touch groups/method); do not encode an invented upstream status identifier. Existing `ReagentData.slipData` is a separate **source** field and must not be confused with character `slip_data`. This local grouping is an adapter over target capabilities, not a claim SS14 serializes a field named `slip_data`.

Bind the SAME `moonstation14:human` prototype to a real player character and at least one real Minecraft villager NPC. Explicit server binding adapters for player-character load/spawn and Minecraft villager spawn/load are permitted; both must attach the exact same key. Gameplay policy comes from the character prototype, never a Player/villager branch. Do not use a separate `MobCivilian` profile, and do not claim `MobHuman` is a vanilla Minecraft villager: the villager is a Minecraft NPC runtime host for the local HUMAN prototype. The long-term invariant is that controller possession or changes never redefine prototype identity or policy; implementing possession/control transfer, or testing a live transfer, is not required in this sprint. Keep records as data, runtime state in owned attachments/status systems, and execution in systems; no reflection, arbitrary maps, runtime class policy, or prototype-ID code branches.

The prototype defines target capabilities/policy: slip susceptibility, intrinsic `NoSlip`, status susceptibility/ability to receive stun, standing/prone eligibility, reactive target groups/methods, and applicable action policy. Source/reagent prototypes define source slipperiness and weighted minimum speed, friction, launch multiplier, stun time, knockdown time, and super-slippery behavior. Eligibility and outcome therefore arise from **both** target capabilities and slippery source/reagent data, plus current runtime conditions and cancellable attempt gates. Neither side can be replaced by a `Player`/NPC class test. Do not clamp source outcome values into a novel character-bounded mapping: follow SS14 semantics unless verified Minecraft constraints necessitate owner-approved deviation.

Keep a minimal extensible no-slip/attempt capability hook for future equipment protection. No equipment integration in this sprint. Character-intrinsic no-slip must be testable. Validate source durations/multipliers/weights/thresholds and all character policy data; reject invalid/nonfinite/negative or unknown behavior-bearing values rather than silently coercing.

1. Verify actual Minecraft server contact/step hooks before choosing one; do not presume SS14 fixtures/intersection events or use raw always-on `entityInside` effects. 3. Apply upstream attempt gates: source/target capability, standing/knockdown/super-slippery behavior, target no-slip/slow response, and cancellable source attempt. Equipment remains out of scope. Failed gates do not produce slip effects or consume solution.
4. On admitted attempt, raise the slip event at the upstream point: **after target/source attempt gates and before velocity/sliding/status changes**. Then apply source-weighted launch/stun/knockdown/friction and related supported effects as verified locally. Event callback exceptions do not imply whole-operation rollback; this operation crosses callbacks and mutable state.
5. Use server random for the independent 50% reactive roll. A failed pre-split target eligibility gate, chance, or source solution resolution consumes nothing. Pinned behavior performs the split before per-reagent group/method filtering, so that later mismatch suppresses that reagent's effect, not its share of the split. A successful slip may still have no touch effect due to those ordinary dispatch filters.
